# Less than a year with AI: learning, building, creating

I first approached AI as something to explore. In less than a year, my projects expanded from questions and experiments to tangible work: Linux troubleshooting and learning notes, tools for everyday tasks, personal software prototypes, documented workflows, and a science-fiction setting explored through writing, illustration and music.

What changed was not an instant acquisition of expertise. It was the ability to investigate a problem, break it into steps, test suggestions, understand failures, and keep what was actually validated. AI makes exploration faster; the human still decides the goal, assesses the evidence and approves the result.

The Linux work became a growing manual: practical solutions, failed approaches, verification steps, and lessons preserved for future use. This repository shares a privacy-safe, curated educational edition rather than exposing the real environment.

The creative work on *La Trame* has similar constraints: recurring characters, visual identity, world consistency and narrative continuity. An appealing image is not automatically an approved part of the work.

The common method is **construct → verify → correct → approve → hand over documented lessons (RETEX)**. The notes make work across different conversations traceable by referring back to persistent files, not by assuming perfect AI memory.

AI does not replace my ideas or decisions. It helps me turn them into things I can build.
